Travel tips & FAQ

Simple answers about visiting and studying these sites.

Spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) are the most pleasant months. Summers are very hot in the desert cities (Yazd, and even Isfahan), while winters can be cold in Tehran. For this project, the good news is that the sites can be looked up at any time — no plane ticket required!

Absolutely. Four of the five featured locations are UNESCO World Heritage Sites (Persepolis, Naqsh-e Jahan, Golestan Palace and the historic city of Yazd), and Eram Garden belongs to the UNESCO "Persian Gardens" group. They are among the most celebrated treasures in the country.

Not to enjoy them! Each site has explanatory signs and local guides are widely available. For studying at home, the landmark pages on this website summarize the main ideas in plain language — plenty for a class presentation.

Dress modestly: shoulders and knees covered, and a headscarf for women (taken outside the buildings). Shoes are removed at the entrance. It is always respectful to be quiet inside a place of worship, even when it is crowded with tourists.

The classic route is Tehran → Kashan → Isfahan → Shiraz (with Persepolis as a day trip from Shiraz), then north to Yazd. Buses, trains and domestic flights are cheap and reliable. For nomads inside the browser, the navigation bar gets you between all ten pages instantly.

UNESCO lists sites of "outstanding universal value." Iran currently has more than 20, which reflects both the length of its history and how well many monuments, gardens and cities have survived for millennia. The listing also helps with funding and protection.
