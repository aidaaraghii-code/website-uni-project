Tehran - Golestan Palace

The dazzling royal complex of the Qajar dynasty, in Tehran.

Overview

Where old Tehran began

The Golestan Palace ("Palace of Roses") is a complex of halls and buildings that served as the royal residence of the Qajar kings in the 19th century. It sits in the old center of Tehran, behind a long garden wall, and blends traditional Persian design with European-influenced flourishes that were fashionable at the time.

Because the Qajars were among the first Iranian rulers to embrace photography, the palace also holds a remarkable archive of early photographs — its oldest production dates to the mid-1800s. In 2013 the palace became a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Inside the palace

Mirrors, marble and the Peacock Throne

The heart of the palace is a cluster of famous halls:

  • Shams-ol-Emareh — the tall "Sun Building" with two towers, one of the first buildings in Tehran to get a clock.
  • Mirror Hall (Talar-e Ayeneh) — walls and ceiling covered in mirror-work, a favorite for royal photographs.
  • Marble Throne (Talar-e Marmar) — an opulent throne carved from a single block of yellow marble, where coronations took place.
  • The Peacock Throne — a jeweled throne (notably used at the coronation of the last Qajar king, and later kept by the Pahlavi dynasty).

The palace grounds are a calm green oasis — roses (the garden's very reason for its name), fountains and sweet-orange trees — in the middle of one of the busiest areas of Tehran.

Good to know

Visiting notes

  • The palace is a 10-minute walk from Panzdah-e Khordad metro station.
  • Photography rules vary room by room — the mirror hall asks for no flash.
  • Plan about two hours to see the main halls comfortably.
  • Entry tickets can be combined to visit multiple halls at once.

Key facts

  • Location: Old center of Tehran, Iran
  • Built: 19th century, under the Qajar dynasty
  • Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2013
  • Famous for: mirror halls and the Marble Throne
  • Collection: among the first photographs ever taken in Iran
