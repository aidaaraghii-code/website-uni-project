Yazd

The windcatcher city and cradle of Zoroastrian culture.

Overview

Living in a desert, at 800 metres

Surrounded by mountains and desert, Yazd is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world. Its old town is a maze of narrow lanes, adobe (mud-brick) houses, covered bazaars and qanat underground water channels — all built to survive one of Iran's most extreme climates.

Because the city has kept its traditional way of building, visitors can wander streets that feel frozen in time. UNESCO listed the whole historic city as a World Heritage Site in 2017.

The two icons

Windcatchers (badgirs)

The most distinctive sight in Yazd is the badgir — a tall tower on top of houses that works as an ancient air conditioner. Openings in the tower catch even the faintest breeze and channel it down into a wind-catching shaft that often runs over a pool of water, cooling the air before it reaches the rooms below. When modern visitors ask how Yazd stayed cool before fans, the answer is simple: the towers did it for centuries.

The Zoroastrian Fire Temple

Yazd is the spiritual center of Zoroastrianism, one of the world's oldest religions. The Fire Temple of Yazd holds a sacred fire that is said to have been burning continuously for more than 1,500 years. Visitors can see the flame behind a glass pane in the main hall, surrounded by a beautiful sunken garden with cypress trees and a lotus pool.

Good to know

More to see in Yazd

  • The Jameh Mosque — one of Iran's tallest entrance arches (two minarets visible from across the city).
  • Amir Chakhmakh Complex — a dramatic multi-storey square with a sunken courtyard.
  • Dowlat Abad Garden — home of the tallest windcatcher in Iran.
  • The Towers of Silence — hilltop Zoroastrian burial structures a little outside the city.
  • Qanat tours — guided trips into the ancient underground water tunnels.

Key facts

  • Location: Central desert of Iran
  • UNESCO: Historic City of Yazd — listed in 2017
  • Famous for: windcatchers, mud-brick architecture and Zoroastrian heritage
  • Sacred fire: burning for over 1,500 years at the Fire Temple
  • Climate: one of the hottest and driest cities in Iran
