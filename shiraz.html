Shiraz — Gardens & Tombs

The city of poetry, roses and cypress trees.

Eram Garden

A Persian paradise garden

Eram Garden (Bagh-e Eram) is one of the finest surviving persian gardens — a style designed to feel like paradise on earth, with running water, cypress and orange trees, and a graceful pavilion at its heart. The garden is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, recognized together with eight other Persian gardens.

Its elegant three-storey pavilion, built in the 19th century, is decorated with tile work and is now part of the Shiraz University botanical garden. Visitors love to walk the long cypress avenue that leads down to the reflecting pool.

Poets' tombs

The tombs of Hafez and Saadi

Shiraz is the city of poetry. Near the garden stand two much-loved monuments dedicated to the city's two greatest poets.

Hafez's tomb (Hafezieh)

Hafez (1325–1390) is Iran's most famous poet, best known for his collected poems, the Divan. His tomb sits in a peaceful garden where people still come to make a wish and ask the Divan for advice (a tradition called fāl-e Hafez). The octagonal tiled pavilion was built in the 1930s, and the tomb is one of the most visited spots in all of Iran.

Saadi's tomb

Saadi (1210–1291) wrote the Gulistan (Rose Garden) and Bustan, books of stories and wisdom studied for centuries. His tomb is a tall blue-tiled building with a beautiful interior, set in another tranquil garden a short distance from Hafez's. Together, the two tombs make Shiraz feel like a living tribute to Persian literature.

Good to know

Unofficial facts of the day

  • Shiraz is also the springboard for visiting Persepolis, about an hour away.
  • The small oranges on Eram's trees are there to be admired, not eaten!
  • Visiting the tombs at sunset is a favourite local tradition.
  • The fāl-e Hafez — asking the poet for advice — works offline too: just open a copy at any page.

Key facts

  • Location: Shiraz, Fars province, southern Iran
  • Eram Garden: UNESCO World Heritage since 2011 (Persian Gardens)
  • Hafez: born ~1325, died 1390 · memorial garden rebuilt in 1935–36
  • Saadi: born ~1210, died 1291 · famous for Gulistan and Bustan
  • Famous for: cypress gardens, poetry and tiled pavilions
